Three steps. Then smugness.

No project files, no timeline wrangling degree. A clip goes in, a captioned clip comes out.

  1. Drop

    Drag a video onto the window. Subya transcribes it right there on your Mac and splits the speech into short caption lines.

  2. Fix

    It heard “biscuit” as “brisket”? Fix it in the list. The preview updates as you type, and it's exactly what gets exported.

  3. Export

    You get a new -captioned.mp4 next to the original, captions drawn into every frame. Your original is never touched.

Small app. Big mouth.

Everything you need to make a clip land, and not a lot else.

One word at a time

Each word pops up alone as it's spoken, the punchy reaction-clip look. A word-level timeline lets you nudge exactly when each one hits.

Put me anywhere

Drag it where it fits

Grab the caption on the video and slide it up or down. It snaps to top, middle and bottom on the way. Resize the text with a slider.

so anyway

A timeline you already know

It works like the trim bar in Photos: a filmstrip with every caption as a block. Drag to move, drag the edges to retime, zoom in for precision.

⌘N

Say what nobody said

Silent stare? Reaction shot? Park the playhead and press ⌘N to add a line nobody says out loud. Videos with no sound at all work too.

Still fits 9:16

Trim it. Crop it.

Keep just the good bit: trim a long video while it's transcribing and only the part you keep gets transcribed. Then crop to 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 or 16:9. Captions stay inside the frame.

Or roll your own

Duplicate any style in Settings and change the lot: any font on your Mac, text and outline colours, outline thickness, a soft shadow, a box behind the text, position, size, caps, and whole lines or one word at a time. With a live preview.

Everything stays on your Mac.

Your clip is transcribed by the speech recognition built into macOS, on your own machine. Nothing is uploaded, there's nothing to sign up for, and Subya doesn't ask for a single permission.

It makes no network calls of its own. Your group chat's secrets are safe. From us, anyway.

Small print: the first time you use a language, macOS may download Apple's speech model for it. And this website has no analytics, no cookies and no trackers either.

Also speaks Terminal.

The subya command-line tool does the same job without the window: transcribe to an SRT file, fix it in any editor, then burn it in. Handy for scripting a batch of clips.

It takes the same styles (including your own), plus --trim, --crop, --words and --small.

It isn't in the download yet: it runs from the source with Swift (Xcode 26). Details in the README.

# get the source
$ git clone https://github.com/duggan/Subya && cd Subya

# transcribe, tidy up the words, burn them in
$ swift run subya transcribe clip.mp4 --srt > clip.srt
$ swift run subya burn clip.mp4 clip-captioned.mp4 --captions clip.srt

# or the lot at once: 15 seconds, vertical, Meme style, small
$ swift run subya burn clip.mp4 out.mp4 \
    --style Meme --trim 1:05-1:20 --crop 9:16 --small

Questions, answered loudly.

Why burn the captions in? Aren't subtitle tracks a thing?

They are, and messaging apps tend to drop or hide them. Captions drawn into the frames can't go missing: if the video plays, the text is there, sound on or off.

Which languages does it understand?

Whatever Apple's on-device transcription supports on your Mac. Subya uses your Mac's language by default, and you can pick another in Settings for clips in a different one.

Intel or Apple silicon?

It's a universal app, and needs macOS 26 (Tahoe) or later. Transcription depends on what macOS supports on your Mac; where it isn't available, you can still add lines by hand.

Why does it need macOS 26?

The on-device transcription Subya is built on arrived in macOS 26. It's also why there's nothing to upload and no permission to grant.

Will it mess with my original video?

Never. Subya always writes a new file (by default name-captioned.mp4, next to the original) and won't overwrite an earlier export either.

My clip has no sound. Now what?

Memes are often silent, so that's fine: the video opens with no captions and you add your own lines wherever you want them.

Is it really free?

Yes. Free to download, no account, no ads, and the source is on GitHub under the GPL-3.0 licence.

Will macOS complain when I open it?

It shouldn't. Subya is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other downloaded app.
